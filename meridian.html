Manufacture Meridian

Every part belongs.

Two hundred and twelve components. One watchmaker’s bench, start to finish — and fifteen days of testing before a calibre earns its name.

Scroll — the bench runs sideways →

01

Calibre M-72

Seventy-two hours from a single winding.

212 components, 31 jewels, beating at 28,800 vibrations per hour. Each calibre is assembled, regulated and cased by one watchmaker, then tested for fifteen days before it earns the name.

A synthesised tick — eight beats a second. The same calibre beats inside Noir, Ivoire and Nuit.
02

Noir Absolu

A dial cut from darkness.

Twelve layers of black lacquer over a sunburst-brushed brass blank. The indices are solid gold appliqué, set by hand, one by one — because a printed index has never caught the light.

03

Or Jaune

Gold that works for a living.

Dauphine hands in solid gold, bevelled and mirror-polished by hand. Gold here is not decoration; it is the part that tells the time.

04

DLC Noir

Black, hardened.

The 40 mm steel case is diamond-like-carbon coated — harder than the steel beneath it — and water-resistant to 5 ATM. Black that does not scratch its way to grey.

“We build the movement first. Everything else is the frame.”
